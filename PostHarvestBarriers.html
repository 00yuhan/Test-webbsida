Post-Harvest Barriers: A Collaborative Study in Siaya, Kenya

PROJECT OVERVIEW

  • ROLE: Industrial designer
  • TIMELINE: 16 weeks (6 in the field)
  • TOOLS: Design Thinking

THE CHALLANGE

Collaborate with smallholder farmers in Siaya to identify post-harvest challenges and designing potential solutions.



Post-Harvest Losses and the Importance of Storage



The farmers we interviewed identified pests, particularly the maize weevil, and mold as their most serious post-harvest challenges. These threats were significant enough to destroy an entire season’s harvest, and many farmers had come to expect that a portion of their crops would inevitably be lost as a result.

Access to effective, long-term storage is therefore critical. It enables farmers to preserve crops for household consumption while also strengthening their economic resilience. Because market prices fluctuate throughout the year, the ability to store produce and sell it later, when demand rises and prices are more favorable, can make the difference between subsistence and a sustainable livelihood.

Latent Need


To protect their harvests from pests such as the maize weevil and from mold, farmers relied on hermetic storage bags. When used correctly, these bags were effective. However, in practice, they presented several challenges. Even the smallest puncture could compromise the airtight seal and render a bag ineffective. In addition, each time a bag was opened for household consumption, the hermetic barrier was temporarily broken, creating an opportunity for pests to be reintroduced.

Sustainability was another concern. The bags were not durable enough for long-term reuse, and many farmers felt economically dependent on repeatedly purchasing them. Over time, this reliance became both costly and frustrating.

In hindsight, it became clear that the core need was not simply a method to prevent pests in storage, but a more sustainable solution, one that would allow farmers to access their crops as needed without increasing the risk of infestation, and without tying them to continual investment in single-use products.
